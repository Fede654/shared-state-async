# Post-expiry behaviour — does a resurrected entry come back for good?

Generated by `experiments/post_expiry.py`. Closes the last open claim in
the divergence chapter: "post-expiry evolution is unmeasured".

## The question

T24 proved the author **can** be handed its own expired entry back:
`sharedstate.cc:866` inserts a missing key before the own-authorship
guard at `:882` can see it. T24 uses an injected echo at a fixed TTL, so
it shows resurrection is possible, not whether it recurs. Two very
different worlds were consistent with it:

- the loop sustains — each round re-inflates the entry and it never dies
- resurrection happens but decays — extinction is delayed, not prevented

## Result: it decays. Extinction in 5 of 5 runs.

5-node chain, one publish and never again, watched for many entry
lifetimes.

| run | kbit | entries | insert TTL | max spread | spread/TTL | resurrections at author | mesh extinct at | absent at end |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| base-0 | 512 | 25 | 24 s | 10 s | 0.42 | 1 (returned at TTL **1 s**) | 55.9 s | yes |
| base-1 | 512 | 25 | 24 s | 6 s | 0.25 | 0 | 44.6 s | yes |
| base-2 | 512 | 25 | 24 s | 12 s | 0.50 | 0 | 53.6 s | yes |
| harsh-1 | 128 | 100 | 24 s | 11 s | 0.46 | 0 | 67.8 s | yes |
| harsh-2 | 128 | 100 | 24 s | 12 s | 0.50 | 0 | 105.4 s | yes |

Extinction at **1.9–4.4 entry lifetimes**, and the entry stayed gone for
the rest of every window (up to 545 s ≈ 23 lifetimes in the longest run).
Harsher impairment, which produces more inflation, **delayed** extinction
(67.8 s and 105.4 s vs 44.6–55.9 s) without preventing it.

## Why it decays — and why the old reasoning was still wrong

The mechanism caught in run base-0, sampled directly:

```
t=29.0  author TTL  2   neighbours 4, 9, 10, 12     author lowest, as documented
t=37.1  author  —       neighbours 5, 7, 8,  8      author has EXPIRED
t=45.4  author TTL  1   neighbours 2, 2, 2,  3      author is BACK — nothing republished
t=55.9  all —                                        extinct, and stays extinct
```

The author really is refreshed by an echo — so **"nothing refreshes the
author's copy" remains withdrawn**, and the guard really is unreachable.
But an echo can only carry the author's own TTL *plus the inflation
accumulated above it*, and that inflation is a fraction of a lifetime
(0.25–0.50 here). So each resurrection donates **less** TTL than the
original insert. The cycle loses energy every round.

Divergence therefore **postpones** extinction rather than defeating it.
That is a different conclusion from both earlier positions: the old
"self-limiting because nothing refreshes it" was right about the outcome
for the wrong reason, and the feared "resurrection may repeat
indefinitely" is not what happens in any run here.

## Does this transfer to the field?

Partly, and the part that does is the qualitative one.

**Transfers.** `spread/TTL` here is 0.25–0.50, overlapping the field
regime implied by the measured rates (0.34–0.70 over a 2431 s lifetime).
The decaying-cycle argument depends on that ratio being below 1, not on
absolute times.

**Does not transfer.** Extinction *times*, and the resurrection *rate*.
A short TTL means few sync rounds per lifetime, so counts here are not
field counts.

## Limits, stated plainly

- **n=5**, one topology (5-node chain), one short TTL (24 s insert),
  two impairment cells. Nothing swept.
- **Sampling can miss brief returns.** Rows are ~8 s apart, so a
  resurrection shorter than that is invisible; the four runs showing 0
  author resurrections are consistent with "none" *or* with "one too
  brief to sample". The extinction result does not depend on this — no
  run had the entry present at the end.
- **The author never republishes.** This is the "author went away" case.
  A live author republishing every interval never reaches expiry at all.
- `spread/TTL` above 1 was never tested, and is where a sustaining loop
  would be expected if one exists.
