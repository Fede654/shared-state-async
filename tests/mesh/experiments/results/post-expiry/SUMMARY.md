# Post-expiry behaviour — resurrection in every treatment run

Generated by `experiments/post_expiry.py`, runs made at commit `bf61fb0`
with clean source and runtime dependencies (five records list only
accumulated generated results as dirty paths — no source file was
modified); analyses re-derived from the stored series after the metric
below was corrected, and each record's `analysis_provenance` block names
the analyser that produced its current analysis fields. Six runs, all
`record_valid: true`, no refusals.

Two earlier attempts were withdrawn in full; see
`../post-expiry-superseded/` for both and why neither is citeable.

## v4 sweep (2026-08-15) — the current citable result

Sixteen further runs under the preregistered-then-amended plan

self-limitation as a property of the protocol.

## Setup

5-node chain, 400 ms delay, 512 kbit, 25 entries, `update_interval` 5 s,
`bleach_ttl` 90 s. The daemon assigns `mBleachTTL + mUpdateInterval + 1`
(`app/shared_state_cli.cc:66`), so the **configured insert TTL is 96 s**.
The first probe lands after publication and reads 94 s; that is
`first_observed_author_ttl`, not the insert TTL, and lifetimes below use
96 s.

The author publishes **once** and never again. Window 480 s = 5 lifetimes.

- **treatment** — 59 sampled rows
- **control** — 3 rows: startup, propagation check, end

Every record cleared the gate: propagation confirmed at all five nodes
before the author's copy could expire, every observation successful,
dependency manifest unchanged.

## Result 1 — resurrection occurred in 3 of 3 treatment runs

The metric first used here counted only present → **sampled absent** →
present, and undercounted: a node's own consecutive samples are ~8.2 s
apart (max 13.6 s), so an entry can expire and be re-adopted entirely
between two looks. It reported 0/1/1.

**A TTL increase cannot hide behind an unsampled absence.** While the
author holds its own key its TTL can only fall — `bleach` decrements,
and a higher own-authored value from a peer is discarded at
`sharedstate.cc:882`. The author publishes once. So an increase in the
author's own TTL is only possible if the key left local state and
returned through the missing-key path at `:866`. It is still a lower
bound: sampling can miss resets too.

The two witness classes are disjoint by construction — a sampled return
requires the preceding author sample to be *absent*, a TTL reset
requires both adjacent samples *present* — so they sum:

| run | sampled absence→return | TTL reset events (deltas) | **evidence ≥** |
|---|---|---|---|
| rep1 | 0 | 2 (+33, +9) | **2** |
| rep2 | 1 | 2 (+37, +15) | **3** |
| rep3 | 1 | 2 (+19, +2) | **3** |

**Every treatment shows at least two resurrections; two show at least
three.** The directly sampled case in rep3, for illustration:

```
t= 93.9  author TTL  6   neighbours 52, 53, 53, 53
t=104.3  author  —       neighbours 43, 45, 46, 48    author EXPIRED
t=112.4  author TTL 34   neighbours 39, 41, 41, 42    author BACK
```

Return TTLs sit far below the 96 s insert, as adopting a neighbour's
already-decayed copy predicts.

The previous cell's "zero resurrections" was a property of that cell: at
`bleach_ttl` 20 the entry reached all five nodes at t≈28 s with the
author already at TTL 3 of 24, leaving no post-expiry window in which a
neighbour still held a copy.

## Result 2 — no presence sampled after ~2 lifetimes

Nothing was sampled present after 184.7 / 189.1 / 210.7 s — roughly
**twice the 96 s configured lifetime** — and all six runs ended with the
entry absent at a valid final row (t≈479–486 s).

That is an endpoint statement, not a proof of self-limitation. Returns
recurred at least twice per run before stopping, and a return shorter
than the ~8 s sampling gap is invisible.

## Result 3 — repeated probing is not needed for the endpoint

All three controls, with **3 rows against the treatment's 59**, also
ended absent. This bounds *repeated* observer load only. It cannot show
whether resurrection occurred in the lightly sampled arm, because three
samples cannot see it.

## What "organic" does and does not mean here

Defensible: the echoes are **peer-generated and not injected** — unlike
T24, nothing in this experiment hands the author an entry.

**Not** established: that this happens in an ordinary, unobserved mesh.
The treatment runs 59 probe rounds occupying the daemon's serial accept
loop, and while an empty probe cannot insert the key, it *can* delay peer
gossip past the author's expiry — which is exactly the timing that opens
the missing-key path. The controls cannot settle this, having no
visibility into resurrection. Separating the two needs passive change
capture: a merge-triggered hook, or packet capture.

## Divergence in this cell

Corrected max spread 41.4 / 42.0 / 49.1 s against the 96 s configured
TTL — `spread/TTL` ≈ **0.43–0.51**.

The author holds the lowest TTL for its own key during the pre-expiry
inflation phase, which is the documented steady state. That is **not**
true throughout: after a resurrection the author can hold more than a
neighbour — rep3 t=154 (author 23, `nodo-suri` 19) and rep1 t=166
(author 5, `tronco` 1). The author-minimum property describes the phase
before expiry, not the whole trajectory.

## What this does NOT establish

- **Anything about production.** interval/TTL here is 5/96 — about 19
  gossip intervals per lifetime — against production's 30/2400, which is
  80. **This lab cell has ~4.2× fewer opportunities per lifetime than
  production**, and that ratio is plausibly what decides whether the
  loop sustains.
- **Self-limitation.** Three runs, one topology, one cell.
- **Continuity of absence.** Per-node sampling gap median 8.17 s, max
  13.6 s; brief returns are invisible, as the corrected metric proves.
- **Simultaneity.** A row is a smear (max span 8.3 s), not a snapshot.
- **Observer-independence of the resurrection rate.**

## Limits

- 3 treatment + 3 control, one topology, one impairment cell.
- The author never republishes — the "author went away" case.
- Controls give endpoints, not trajectories.
