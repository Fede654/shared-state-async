# Post-expiry behaviour — organic resurrection, reproduced

Generated by `experiments/post_expiry.py` at commit `bf61fb0`, on a
clean tree. Six runs, all `record_valid: true`, no refusals.

Two earlier attempts were withdrawn in full; see
`../post-expiry-superseded/` for both and for why neither is citeable.

## Setup

5-node chain, 400 ms delay, 512 kbit, 25 entries, `update_interval` 5 s,
`bleach_ttl` 90 s → **insert TTL 94 s**. The author publishes **once** and
never again. Window 480 s ≈ 5 entry lifetimes.

- **treatment** — sampled every ~8 s (59 valid rows)
- **control** — sampled 3 times: startup, propagation check, end

Every run had to clear the gate before its record counted: propagation
confirmed at all five nodes, *before* the author's copy could expire;
every observation successful; dependency manifest unchanged.

## Result 1 — organic resurrection is real, in 2 of 3 treatment runs

The author regained its own key **after local expiry, without
publishing**, in rep2 and rep3. rep3, sampled directly:

```
t= 93.9  author TTL  6   neighbours 52, 53, 53, 53
t=104.3  author  —       neighbours 43, 45, 46, 48    author EXPIRED
t=112.4  author TTL 34   neighbours 39, 41, 41, 42    author BACK
t=137.5  author TTL 12   neighbours 25, 29, 30, 30    decaying again
```

rep2 shows the same shape at t=165 → 172.5, returning at TTL 5.

Return TTLs were **34 s and 5 s**, both far below the 94 s insert —
consistent with adopting a neighbour's already-decayed copy, which is
what `sharedstate.cc:866` does for a key the author no longer holds.

This is what the previous cell could not show. At `bleach_ttl` 20 the
entry reached all five nodes at t≈28 s with the author already at TTL 3
of 24: propagation took as long as the entry lived, leaving no
post-expiry window in which a neighbour still held a copy. Zero
resurrections there was a property of the *cell*, not of the daemon.

## Result 2 — it did not sustain in any run

| arm | run | resurrections at author | no later sampled presence from | absent at end |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| treatment | rep1 | 0 | 184.7 s | yes (t=479.1) |
| treatment | rep2 | 1 (TTL 5) | 189.1 s | yes (t=478.6) |
| treatment | rep3 | 1 (TTL 34) | 210.7 s | yes (t=480.7) |
| control | rep1 | 0 | — | yes (t=485.3) |
| control | rep2 | 0 | — | yes (t=485.1) |
| control | rep3 | 0 | — | yes (t=485.5) |

Every run ended with the entry absent, and nothing was sampled present
after ~185–211 s — roughly **twice the 94 s insert TTL**. So resurrection
extended the entry's life in the mesh beyond its own nominal TTL, but did
not make it permanent in any run observed here.

## Result 3 — the disappearance is not caused by sustained probing

All three controls, carrying **3 probes instead of ~37**, also ended
absent. Sustained observer load is therefore not what made the entry
disappear. This bounds the *repeated* load only: whatever a single probe
does is present in both arms, and the control cannot say *when* the entry
went, having only three samples.

## Divergence in this cell

Corrected max spread 41–49 s in the treatment against a 94 s TTL —
`spread/TTL` ≈ 0.44–0.52. The author held the lowest TTL for its own key
throughout, as documented elsewhere.

## What this does NOT establish

- **Anything about production.** interval/TTL here is 5/94; production is
  30/2400, about four times fewer gossip opportunities per lifetime, and
  that ratio is plausibly what decides whether the loop sustains. No
  production-TTL run has been attempted.
- **That resurrection cannot sustain.** Three runs, one topology, one
  cell. Two resurrections were observed and both decayed; that is not a
  proof of self-limitation, and the earlier claim of one is withdrawn.
- **Continuous absence.** Nodes are sampled every ~8 s (max row span
  8.3 s), so a resurrection shorter than that gap is invisible. "No later
  sampled presence" means exactly that, not continuous quiescence.
- **Simultaneity.** A row is a smear of up to 8.3 s, not a snapshot.

## Limits

- 3 treatment + 3 control, one topology, one impairment cell.
- The author never republishes — this is the "author went away" case.
- Controls give endpoints, not trajectories.
